**Industry**: ACTIVE, lean long (breadth +0.73) · 15 ranked · 12 web query(ies) · model glm-5.3-flash

NVDA is the strongest long in the group: the only mega-cap with accelerating beat sizes (3.46% to 6.16% across the last four quarters), unanimous upward revisions and a below-median multiple to carry into the quarter ending 2026-09-30. The best secondary long is MPWR, whose FY1 was marked up 13.5% in 30 days on the back of a 10.5% beat with zero downgrades. The weakest member is CRUS: the only name with materially negative revisions (-5.3%), a beat record decaying from 21.9% to 1.4%, and four downgrades in 30 days — the group's clearest short.

**Why this group is not COLD (upside):** The forward evidence is dominated by AI datacenter demand outrunning supply: Broadcom's Q3 FY26 print (reported this week) showed $29.6B revenue up 86% y/y with AI revenue of $16.7B and a stated FY27 AI target of $115B — nearly 2x FY26 — plus a Singapore fab to relieve the substrate bottleneck, and Lam Research explicitly guided that strong growth extends into 2027 as AI spending accelerates. That means the next prints in this group should report continued double-digit y/y datacenter revenue growth, capacity-constrained order books, and guidance raises rather than in-line guides — MRVL already filed a Q2 FY27 guide to $2.7B (+35% y/y) on 800G/1.6T optics and custom XPU demand, and NVDA's next quarter should carry the accelerating beat pattern (3.46%->6.16%) forward on $96.2B of Q2 revenue up 106%. The ISM backdrop corroborates: Manufacturing PMI 54.6 with new orders 53.7 and Electrical Equipment expanding, so the analog/industrial names (MPWR +13.5% 30d revisions, MCHP +14.4%, ADI's record $4.02B quarter led by Data Center and Industrial) have a second, broader demand leg, and onsemi's own call that auto demand has bottomed removes the main drag on that cluster. The divergence is CRUS (and to a lesser degree QCOM): CRUS's -5.3% FY1 revisions and collapsed beat size reflect a single-customer audio/content-id mix problem, idiosyncratic to handset content attach rather than a group-wide demand break — its own Q2 guide of $510-570M with secured GlobalFoundries wafer capacity for 2027-2028 signals stabilization, not deterioration. The one caution inside the long side is SNDK, where revisions are rising (+22.1% 90d) but beat sizes decelerated sharply (74.97%->13.72%), so the memory leg needs its next print to re-confirm; that is a member-level flag, not a group read. This group flips to COLD only if the AI capex chain visibly cracks: Broadcom's FY27 AI target being walked back, Lam/ASML cutting 2027 equipment outlook, or NVDA/MRVL guiding flat-to-down next quarter — none of which the September-October print calendar currently points toward.

## How to read this
- **One pass, not one per name.** Every member of the industry was ranked against the others in a single call, from the same measured inputs — the last print's EPS surprise and the four-quarter beat record, the name's own FY1 consensus change over 90 and 30 days with the analyst up/down counts, consensus growth, filed guidance from the latest EDGAR pack, and forward P/E, PEG and P/S against the industry median. There is no per-name deep dive behind these rows.
- **The factual columns are computed, not written.** Latest earnings surprise, print setup and the multiples in the guidance/valuation cell are rendered from the cached data; the model supplies only the guidance read, the ordering and the narrative. A number in those columns is measured.
- **Fundamentals only, no price action.** No technicals, no moving averages, no momentum or trend reads, no support or resistance, no price targets — the pass is never shown a price. The third column is the print SETUP (how expectations are moving into the print), which is where a trend column would otherwise sit.
- **Valuation is a hurdle and an asymmetry, never a score.** A premium multiple is usually the market correctly paying for better growth or durability, and a discount is usually a correct mark-down, so the level of the multiple is not itself bullish or bearish. It counts in two ways: a high multiple needs a bigger beat and a raise to hold, and the multiple sizes the damage if the driver it assumes breaks. Cheapness is not a catalyst inside three months.
- **The best long and the best short are separate calls.** Each gets its own thesis, catalyst, setup and risk rather than a share of one ranking narrative, because they are two different trades. The short leads wherever the industry's estimate breadth is flat or negative: in a group that is not rising, the useful answer is which member's expectations get cut, and the tactical line leads with it. "None in this industry" is a real answer — an admitted absence beats an invented short.
- **The side is the pass's call, not the revision rule's.** A ⚠ marks a name whose assigned side opposes the direction of its own FY1 revisions — the pass thinks it knows something the estimate trend has not caught up with, and the risk column has to say what.
- Commentary, not a gate: nothing downstream reads this ranking to include or drop a name.
